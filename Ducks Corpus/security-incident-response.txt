1. PURPOSE

This document explains how Ducks on the Pond Cards protects the Application
and the data collectors enter, how to report a security problem, and what we
do when something goes wrong.

Related documents: the Privacy Notice (privacy-notice.txt), the Terms of Use
(terms-of-use.txt) and the Customer Service Policy

2. HOW THE APPLICATION IS PROTECTED

2.1 Access to data

  - Row-level security in the database: each collector can change only their
    own cards and wants, and can see only trades they are part of.
  - Only admins can see all collection data, use the Admin page, and manage
    market price data.
  - Other collectors never see paid prices, listed prices, acquired dates,
    private wants, non-tradeable cards or sold records.
  - Test login can be switched off per environment, so visitors can browse
    but not make changes.

2.2 Data in transit and storage

  - All traffic to the Application uses HTTPS.
  - Connections to the database are encrypted, and the database server's
    certificate is checked (sslmode=verify-full).
  - Database credentials and other secrets are kept in the hosting
    platform's environment settings, never in the code.

2.3 Logs and error messages

  - Request logs record the path only. Query strings are dropped, because
    they can carry tokens or email addresses.
  - The logger removes values whose names suggest secrets (passwords, tokens,
    cookies, sessions, API keys, authorization headers, email addresses,
    request bodies, card numbers, connection strings) and strips passwords
    from URLs, before anything is written.
  - Error messages shown to users never include database host or account
    details. Unexpected errors are logged as "action_failed".

2.4 Environments and changes

  - Development and production use separate database branches.
  - Development scripts refuse to run against the production database.
  - Schema changes are made through versioned migrations, and each production
    migration is approved before it runs.
  - Third-party libraries are updated when security advisories are published.

2.5 What the Application does not hold

No passwords (the test release has no personal accounts), payment card
numbers or bank details. This limits what an attacker could take.

2.6 Known gaps [TO CONFIRM plan and dates]

  - Shared test users instead of personal accounts and real sign-in.
  - No rate limiting on the search endpoint.
  - No automated alerts on unusual activity. Logs are reviewed by hand.


3. REPORTING A SECURITY PROBLEM

3.1 How to report

Email security@ducksonthepondcards.com [TO CONFIRM]. Include:

  - what you found and where (page or API route);
  - steps to reproduce it;
  - what an attacker could do with it;
  - your contact details, if you want a reply.

Do not include other collectors' data in your report, beyond what is needed
to show the problem.

3.2 What we promise

  - We acknowledge reports within 2 business days.
  - We give an initial assessment within 5 business days.
  - We keep you updated until it is fixed, and credit you if you want.
  - We will not take legal action against good-faith research that follows
    section 3.3.

3.3 Rules for testing

  - Test only with the test users, or with an account you own.
  - Do not access, change or delete other collectors' data.
  - Do not run denial-of-service tests, spam, or automated scans that put
    heavy load on the Application.
  - Do not test the hosting or database providers' own systems.
  - Give us reasonable time to fix a problem before you disclose it publicly.
    [TO CONFIRM, for example 90 days]

3.4 Collectors

If you think someone has used your account, or you see another collector's
private data, contact support straight away. Support treats it as Urgent
(Customer Service Policy section 5).


4. WHAT COUNTS AS AN INCIDENT

A security incident is any event that puts the confidentiality, integrity or
availability of the Application or its data at risk. Examples:

  - Private data shown to the wrong collector (paid prices, private wants,
    someone else's trades).
  - A card's ownership changed without a valid trade or admin decision.
  - A leaked secret, such as a database connection string.
  - Unauthorized admin access.
  - The Application or database unavailable because of an attack.
  - A vulnerable third-party library being exploited.

6. RESPONDING TO AN INCIDENT

6.1 Roles

  Incident lead     Runs the response, makes decisions, keeps a timeline.
  Engineer          Investigates, contains and fixes.
  Communications    Updates collectors, support staff and anyone else who
                    needs to know. In a small team one person may hold more
                    than one role.

6.2 Steps

  1. Detect and report. Anyone who sees a possible incident tells the
     incident lead at once. Support escalates any suspected data exposure as
     Urgent.
  2. Assess. Confirm what happened, choose a severity, and open an incident
     record with a timeline.
  3. Contain. Stop it getting worse. Options include:
       - switching test login off, so the Application is read-only;
       - rotating the database password and any other secrets that may have
         leaked, and updating them in the hosting platform's settings;
       - rolling back to the last good deployment;
       - pausing the affected page or API route.
  4. Preserve evidence. Save the relevant server logs and the deployment and
     database state before they expire or change. Search logs by time, route
     and codes such as action_failed and request_failed.
  5. Fix. Correct the cause, test it on the development branch, and deploy.
     Restore wrongly changed records from a database backup or point-in-time
     restore where needed. [TO CONFIRM backup retention]
  6. Recover. Turn features back on, check the health endpoint and the main
     pages, and watch the logs closely for 48 hours.
  7. Notify (section 7).
  8. Review (section 8).


7. TELLING PEOPLE

  - Affected collectors: without undue delay once a SEV 1 or SEV 2 incident
    involving their data is confirmed, and within 72 hours at most. [TO
    CONFIRM against applicable law]
  - What we tell them: what happened, what data was involved, what we have
    done, what they should do, and how to contact us.
  - Regulators: where the law requires it. [TO CONFIRM by jurisdiction]
  - Support staff: briefed before collectors are told, so they can answer
    questions.
  - Reporters of the problem: told when it is fixed.

We do not ask collectors to confirm passwords or payment details by email
after an incident. Any such email is not from us.


8. AFTER AN INCIDENT

Within 10 business days of a SEV 1 or SEV 2 incident, the team writes a short
review:

  - a timeline of what happened;
  - the cause, and why it was not caught sooner;
  - what went well and what did not;
  - actions to stop it happening again, each with an owner and a date.

Reviews are blameless: the aim is to fix systems and processes, not to blame
people. Agreed changes are made to this document, the Customer Service Policy
or the code as needed.
