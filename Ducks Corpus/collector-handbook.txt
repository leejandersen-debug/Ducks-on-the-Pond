DUCKS ON THE POND CARDS
COLLECTOR HANDBOOK

Version: November 2026

This handbook is about collecting and trading well with Ducks on the Pond
Cards. For button-by-button steps, see the User's Guide (users-guide.txt). For
rules, see the Terms of Use (terms-of-use.txt) and the Customer Service Policy
(customer-service-policy.txt). Words you may not know are explained in the
Glossary (glossary.txt).


1. WELCOME TO THE POND

Ducks on the Pond Cards is a shared notebook for sports card collectors. You
keep a record of the cards you own, say which ones you would trade, list the
cards you are hunting for, and the Application points you to collectors who
have what you want.

The Application keeps records. It does not hold money, ship cards or check
that a card is genuine. The cards, and the trust between collectors, are
yours.

The name comes from baseball: "ducks on the pond" means runners on base,
waiting to be brought home. Your wishlist is your ducks on the pond.


2. SETTING UP YOUR COLLECTION

2.1 Start with the catalog

Every card you own is a copy of a catalog variant. Before you add a card,
check that its set, card and parallel are in the Catalog. If not, add them
(User's Guide section 5). The catalog is shared, so:

  - Look through the Sets table before adding a set, to avoid duplicates.
  - Use the manufacturer's own names, for example "Topps" and "Chrome".
  - Enter the print run on numbered parallels. Without it, serial numbers
    cannot be checked.
  - If you spot a mistake you cannot fix, report it (Customer Service Policy
    section 8).

2.2 Add your cards carefully

You cannot edit a copy's grade, serial or paid price after it is added, so
check before you click "Add card":

  - The right variant: Base, Refractor, Gold Refractor and so on, and Auto or
    Relic where it applies.
  - The serial number exactly as stamped, without the print run. For 23/50,
    enter 23.
  - For a graded card, both the grading company and the grade, plus the cert
    number from the slab label.
  - What you paid and when. Your reports are only as good as these numbers.

If you make a mistake, delete the copy and add it again.

2.3 A good routine

  - Add new cards on the day they arrive.
  - Use condition notes for things a buyer or trader would ask about: soft
    corners, surface scratches, off-centre printing.
  - Mark cards sold as soon as they sell, so your reports stay right and the
    serial is freed for the new owner.


3. CARD CONDITION AND GRADING BASICS

3.1 Raw or graded

A raw card is ungraded. A graded card has been sealed in a plastic case (a
slab) by a grading company, which gives it a grade from 1 to 10. The
Application supports PSA, BGS, SGC and CGC, in half-grade steps.

3.2 What graders look at

  - Centering: how evenly the image sits within the borders.
  - Corners: sharp, or rounded and soft.
  - Edges: clean, or chipped and rough.
  - Surface: scratches, print lines, dents, stains.

3.3 Describing a raw card honestly

Use plain words in condition notes, and describe the worst flaw first. For
example: "Light whitening on the back top-left corner; front clean; centering
about 60/40 left to right." Never call a raw card "gem mint" or give it a
number grade of your own. A grade means a grading company's grade.

3.4 Protecting your cards

  - Penny sleeve and top loader for raw cards of any value.
  - Keep cards away from sunlight, damp and heat.
  - Handle by the edges.


4. TRADING WELL

4.1 Before you offer

  - Turn on Tradeable only for cards you have in hand and would really part
    with. Collectors will make offers based on it.
  - Use public wants. They are how other collectors find you.
  - Set a minimum grade and a maximum price on wants, so matches are worth
    your time.

4.2 Making an offer

  - Start from the Wishlist page: "propose a trade" fills in the collector and
    card for you.
  - Say in the message how you would like to exchange the cards, and how you
    will pack them.
  - Offer fair value. Recent sales of the same card and grade are a better
    guide than asking prices.
  - Do not be put off by a counteroffer. It is how trades get agreed.

4.3 Exchanging the cards

  - Agree who sends first. For a first trade with someone, sending at the same
    time, or meeting at a card show, is common.
  - Pack cards in a sleeve and top loader, between card board, in a padded
    envelope or small box. Use tracked post for anything of value.
  - When the cards arrive, check the set, card number, parallel, serial,
    grade and cert number against the trade.
  - Only then click "Complete (cards exchanged)". Completing moves the
    records and cannot be undone by you.

4.4 After the trade

Cards you receive arrive with Tradeable off and no paid price. Add notes, and
turn Tradeable on if you want to offer them again.

4.5 When something goes wrong

Talk to your trading partner first. If you cannot sort it out within 7 days,
contact support with the details listed in section 4 of the Customer Service
Policy.


5. SELLING

The Application does not handle sales, but it helps you keep track:

  - Enter a listed price to show a card in your For sale report. Other
    collectors cannot see listed prices.
  - Record the sale price and date with "Mark sold". The Sold report then
    shows your profit.
  - Arrange payment and delivery yourself, using a method that protects
    both sides.


6. SERIAL NUMBERS AND DISPUTES

Only one unsold copy of a numbered variant can hold each serial number. If
yours is already taken, the card goes to an admin. Use the "Note for admin"
box when you add the card, because it cannot be changed later. Say how you
know your number is right, for example a cert number. The full process is in
the Serial-Number Dispute Procedure (serial-number-dispute-procedure.txt).


7. STAYING SAFE

  - Ducks on the Pond Cards staff will never ask for your password, payment
    card number or bank details.
  - Keep payment and address details out of trade messages and wishlist
    notes. Other collectors can see public wants.
  - Be wary of a collector who asks you to always send first, pushes for an
    unusual payment method, or offers far above market value.
  - Report suspicious behaviour, or a card you think is counterfeit, to
    support.


8. USING YOUR REPORTS

  - Summary: a quick count of what you own, what is listed, and what you have
    sold, with totals.
  - Inventory: every unsold card, with what you paid and the market price
    where one is known.
  - For sale: listed cards and the margin over what you paid.
  - Sold: sales, newest first, with profit.

Totals include only cards with the prices filled in, so record what you pay
for every card.


9. COLLECTOR ETIQUETTE

  - Reply to offers, even with a decline. Do not leave offers hanging.
  - Cancel an offer you no longer want, rather than ignoring it.
  - Be kind in messages. Everyone was a new collector once.
  - Keep your Tradeable cards current. A card that is not really available
    wastes other collectors' time.
