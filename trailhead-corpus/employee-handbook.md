## 1. Welcome & About Trailhead

### How we started

Trailhead Supply Co. opened its doors in March 2014 on Pine Street in Pinedale, Wyoming. It was founded by two climbing partners, Greta Lindqvist and Cal Okafor, who had spent more weekends than they could count chasing routes in the Winds and arguing, on long drives home, about the gear shop they wished existed.

The shop they kept describing was simple. It would carry gear that actually held up. The people behind the counter would have used the things they sold, in real weather, on real trips, and they'd tell you the truth about what worked. And when something broke — because gear always eventually breaks — the shop wouldn't shrug and point you to a new one off the shelf. It would help you fix it.

They couldn't find that shop in their corner of Wyoming, so they built it. Greta handled the front of the house and the business side; Cal handled operations, fulfillment, and the logistics of getting good gear into a small mountain town. They signed the lease on the Pine Street space, painted the walls themselves, and stocked the first shelves with brands they'd personally vouch for. The first month was lean. The second month was leaner. But the customers who came in came back, and they brought friends, and slowly the shop became the thing Greta and Cal had pictured on those drives home.

### How we've grown

Trailhead has grown a piece at a time, always in response to what customers actually asked for rather than what looked good on a growth chart.

- **2014 — The storefront.** The Pine Street shop opened. A single retail floor, a tiny back office, and the two founders doing nearly every job in the building.
- **2018 — The online store.** Customers from out of town kept calling to ask whether we'd ship. Enough of them asked that we built an online store at trailheadsupply.co and started fulfilling orders beyond Pinedale.
- **2020 — In-house gear repair.** We'd always tried to point people toward repairs instead of replacements, but in 2020 we made it official and brought repair in-house. We set up a proper bench, hired a technician, and started fixing boots, packs, tents, and zippers under our own roof.
- **2022 — Seasonal rentals.** Visitors heading into the Winds for a few days didn't always want to buy a full kit. So we added seasonal rentals — packs, tents, sleeping systems, and more — so people could get outside without buying gear they'd use once.

By 2026, Trailhead is a team of about 12 people. We're still independent. We're still on Pine Street. And the founding idea — a shop run by people who use the gear and stand behind it — is still the whole point.

Each addition reshaped the shop a little. The online store turned a one-room operation into a business with a packing bench and a shipping rhythm. The repair bench changed what kind of help we could offer — and changed what kind of people we hired, because suddenly we needed hands that could fix things, not just sell them. Rentals brought a wave of first-timers through the door, people who'd never owned a tent in their lives, and taught us how to outfit a complete beginner for a few nights out and send them off feeling capable instead of overwhelmed. The shop you're joining is the sum of those decisions, and it's still changing.

### Where we sit in Pinedale

Pinedale is a gateway town. People pass through on their way into the Wind River Range, the Bridger Wilderness, and the lakes and trailheads scattered across the high country. That shapes our customer base: locals who know exactly what they want, and visitors who need a knowledgeable hand and maybe a forgotten piece of gear before they head up. We get both, and we serve both the same way — straight answers, good gear, no condescension.

Being the local shop comes with responsibility. When someone asks about conditions on a pass or whether a route is in, they're trusting that the person behind the counter actually knows. A lot of the time, that person is you. We'll help you build that knowledge — it's part of working here — but the expectation that we know our own backyard is real and it's important.

### "Repair, don't replace."

That phrase is on our wall, on our packaging, and at the center of how we think. It started as a practical promise to customers: before you spend money on something new, let's see if the thing you already own can be saved. It turned out to be good business, good for our customers' wallets, and good for the mountains we all came here for.

It also shapes how we work internally. We take care of our tools, our space, and our gear. We fix the squeaky cart wheel instead of buying a new cart. We resole the demo boots. When you see something around the shop that could be repaired rather than tossed, that instinct is exactly right — it's the company in miniature.

## 2. Our Mission & Values

**Our mission** is to help people get outside with confidence by selling gear that lasts, repairing the gear they already own, and giving honest advice from people who've been out there.

We hire for skill, but we keep people who live our values. These are the values:

**1. Tell the truth about gear.**
We don't upsell. If a cheaper item is the right call, we say so. If a customer doesn't need a thing, we tell them. Our credibility is the most valuable inventory we have, and it's the one thing we can't reorder.

**2. Repair before replace.**
This is our tagline and our habit. We extend the life of gear whenever we reasonably can — for customers and for ourselves. Waste is expensive and it's avoidable.

**3. Know the terrain.**
We're a local shop in a place people travel to. We're expected to know the trails, the conditions, the seasons, and what they demand. Curiosity about the outdoors isn't a nice-to-have here; it's part of the job.

**4. Take care of each other.**
We're a small crew. When someone's slammed, you step in. When someone makes a mistake, you help them fix it. Nobody here wins alone.

**5. Keep it welcoming.**
The outdoors can feel like a club with a secret handshake. We work against that. Beginners get the same patience and respect as the customer buying their fifth ice axe. Everyone starts somewhere.

**6. Mind the details.**
A mispriced item, a sloppy repair, a messy break room — small things add up, for customers and for the team. We sweat the details because they're what people actually feel.

These aren't posters on a wall. They show up in how we hire, who we keep, and what gets praised in a shift. If you ever wonder whether you made the right call in a gray area, run it past these six. They'll usually point you the right way.

### What you can expect from us

Values run both directions. Here's what the company owes you:

- **Honesty about your job.** You'll know what's expected, how you're doing, and where you stand. No surprises at review time.
- **Fair pay, paid on time.** Every hour you work, paid correctly and on schedule.
- **A safe place to work.** We take safety seriously and fix hazards when you flag them.
- **Respect.** From the founders down. Your ideas get heard, your concerns get taken seriously, and you're treated like an adult.
- **Room to grow.** We promote from within when we can, and we'll invest in you learning the gear, the trades, and the trails.

We're small enough that the people at the top know your name and notice your work. We try hard to make that a feature, not a pressure.

## 4. Employment Basics

### At-will employment

Employment at Trailhead Supply Co. is **at-will**. That means either you or the company may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause and with or without notice, subject to applicable law. Nothing in this handbook, and nothing said by any manager or owner, creates a contract of employment or a guarantee of continued employment for any specific period. We hope you'll stay a long time — but the relationship is at-will.

### Equal employment opportunity

Trailhead is an equal-opportunity employer. We make employment decisions — hiring, pay, scheduling, promotion, discipline, and termination — based on qualifications, performance, and the needs of the business. We do not discriminate on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, age, disability, genetic information, veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by federal, state, or local law.

If you believe you've experienced discrimination, see the Anti-Harassment & Respectful Workplace section for how to raise it. We take these concerns seriously and will not retaliate against anyone for raising one in good faith.

### Employee classifications

Everyone at Trailhead falls into one of these categories. Your offer letter states which one applies to you, and your manager can confirm it.

- **Full-time.** Regularly scheduled for 32 or more hours per week. Full-time employees are eligible for our PTO accrual, paid holidays, and the other benefits described in this handbook.
- **Part-time.** Regularly scheduled for fewer than 32 hours per week. Part-time employees are eligible for some benefits on a prorated basis, as described in the relevant sections.
- **Seasonal.** Hired for a defined busy period — typically our summer rental and visitor season, or a holiday stretch. Seasonal employees are usually classified as non-exempt and are eligible for benefits only where this handbook or law specifically provides.

We also classify each role as **exempt** or **non-exempt** under the Fair Labor Standards Act. Most hourly roles at Trailhead are non-exempt, meaning they're eligible for overtime as described in the Pay & Timekeeping section. Your manager or Nadia Foster can tell you your classification.

### Your first days

We try to make starting here feel welcoming rather than overwhelming. Your first week generally looks like this:

- **Day one** is about people and place. You'll meet the team, get a tour of the floor, the stockroom, the repair bench, and the back office, and get set up with your logins, your timekeeping, and your branded shirt.
- **Your first shifts** pair you with an experienced teammate — often Brett Castellano or another senior associate on the floor — so you learn how we actually do things rather than just reading about them.
- **The gear knowledge** comes over your first weeks, not your first day. Nobody expects you to know every product on day one. We'd much rather you say "let me grab someone who knows this better" than guess at a customer.

You'll also have a 90-day initial period. It's not a trick or a countdown — it's just an honest window for both of us to make sure the fit is right, with check-ins along the way so you're never wondering how it's going. Ask questions the whole time. The people who do best here are the ones who stay curious and aren't afraid to say "I don't know that yet."

### Your employee file

We keep a personnel file for each employee with your contact information, tax and pay forms, your classification, and any records related to your employment. Keep your details current — especially your address, phone number, and emergency contact — by telling your manager or Nadia Foster when something changes. Accurate information matters for your pay, your benefits, and reaching someone if there's ever an emergency.

## 6. Pay & Timekeeping

### Pay schedule

Trailhead pays on a **biweekly** schedule — every two weeks, on Friday. That works out to 26 pay periods a year. Each paycheck covers the two-week period that ended the prior Sunday. If a payday lands on a holiday, you'll typically be paid the business day before.

We strongly prefer direct deposit, and we'll set you up during onboarding. Nadia Foster handles payroll questions; if your check looks wrong, tell your manager and Nadia right away so we can fix it fast.

### Clocking in and out

Non-exempt employees must clock in and out using our timekeeping system for every shift, including clocking out for unpaid meal breaks. This isn't about distrust — it's how we make sure you're paid correctly and how we stay compliant with wage law.

A few rules:

- **Clock in when you start and out when you finish.** Don't clock in early and stand around, and don't keep working off the clock. If you're working, you're on the clock.
- **Only you clock you in.** Never clock in or out for another person, and never ask anyone to do it for you. That's a serious integrity issue.
- **Fix mistakes honestly.** If you forget to clock in, or the system glitches, tell your manager the same day so they can correct your time accurately.

Exempt employees don't clock hourly time but should still keep their manager informed about their schedule and any time away.

### Overtime

Non-exempt employees who work more than 40 hours in a workweek earn overtime at one-and-a-half times their regular rate, as required by law. Our workweek runs Sunday through Saturday for overtime purposes.

**Overtime must be approved in advance by your manager.** We schedule to avoid it, but the rental season and big online order pushes can occasionally require it. If you see your hours heading past 40, flag it to your manager before it happens. Working unapproved overtime can lead to a conversation about scheduling — but to be clear, we always pay you for all the time you actually work, approved or not. The approval rule is about planning, not about withholding pay.

## 7. Paid Time Off & Holidays

This section covers our paid time off (PTO) and paid holidays, and how to request time away.

### PTO accrual

**Full-time employees accrue 15 days of paid time off per year.** PTO accrues steadily across the year as you work, rather than all landing on January 1, so a new hire builds their balance over their first months. Your accrued balance shows up in our timekeeping system, and Nadia Foster can confirm it if you're unsure.

PTO is yours to use for vacations, personal days, appointments, family time — whatever you need. You don't have to explain why. We just ask that you plan ahead and coordinate with your team so the shop stays covered.

A note on carryover and payout: you may carry a reasonable balance into the next year, but we encourage you to actually take your time off — rest is the point. Specifics on carryover limits are confirmed by Nadia Foster and may be adjusted year to year; she'll tell you the current rule.

### Paid holidays

Full-time employees receive **8 paid holidays** per year:

1. New Year's Day
2. Memorial Day
3. Juneteenth
4. Independence Day
5. Labor Day
6. Thanksgiving Day
7. The day after Thanksgiving
8. Christmas Day

When a holiday falls on a day the store is open and you'd normally work, you get the day off paid. Because we're a retail shop, some holidays are among our busier days, and the store may stay open. If you're scheduled to work on a paid holiday, talk with your manager about how that holiday is handled for your role — we make sure you're treated fairly, and we rotate holiday coverage so the same people aren't always working them.

If a holiday falls on a day you don't normally work, it doesn't add an extra paid day; holiday pay applies to your regular scheduled day.

### How to request time off

1. **Give at least two weeks' notice** for planned time off whenever you possibly can. The more lead time, the better — popular weeks in the summer fill up.
2. **Submit the request through your manager.** Tell them the dates and let them confirm coverage. They'll approve it or work with you on alternatives.
3. **Wait for approval before you book anything nonrefundable.** Most requests get approved, but during peak rental and visitor season we can only have so many people out at once, so approvals go roughly first-come, first-served.

We do our best to say yes. When we can't, it's almost always a coverage issue, and your manager will explain and help you find another window.

A few practical notes on time off:

- **Unplanned days happen.** Life isn't always two weeks out. For genuine emergencies or last-minute needs, contact your manager as early as you can and we'll work with you — the two-weeks rule is for planned time, not a wall against real life.
- **Plan around the busy season.** Our summer rental and visitor stretch is when we need the most hands, so long vacations are hardest to grant then. If you've got a big trip in mind, the earlier you raise it, the better your odds.
- **Take your time.** We'd genuinely rather you use your PTO than hoard it. People who never step away get worn down, and worn-down people don't take good care of customers or themselves. Rest is part of doing the job well.
- **Coordinate with your team.** A quick heads-up to the people you work alongside, beyond the formal request, makes coverage smoother and keeps everyone in the loop.

### Part-time and seasonal accrual

The 15-day PTO figure and the 8 paid holidays above apply to full-time employees.

- **Part-time employees** accrue PTO on a prorated basis tied to the hours they work, rather than the full 15-day amount. Paid-holiday eligibility is also prorated. Nadia Foster can tell you your specific accrual rate.
- **Seasonal employees** generally do not accrue PTO or receive paid holidays, given the short, defined nature of seasonal work, except where required by law. Your offer details will say what applies to your seasonal role.

## 8. Sick Leave & Leaves of Absence

### Sick leave

If you're sick, stay home. We're a small team that handles gear, helps customers face-to-face, and shares a break room — pushing through a contagious illness to come in helps no one. We'd much rather cover your shift than spread something through the crew or to customers.

When you're going to miss a shift for illness:

- **Tell your manager as early as you can** — ideally before your shift starts, by phone or text, not by quietly not showing up. Early notice gives us the best shot at covering the floor.
- **Use your PTO** for sick days; our paid time off is designed to cover illness as well as vacation, so you don't lose pay when you're under the weather.
- **For longer illnesses,** keep your manager updated, and we'll figure out a plan together. If you'll be out more than a few days, we may ask for a doctor's note before you return, mainly so we know you're cleared and so we can support a smooth comeback.

We comply with all applicable Wyoming and federal sick-leave and wage requirements; where the law provides more than this policy, the law governs.

### Leaves of absence

Sometimes life requires more than a sick day — a serious health condition, the arrival of a child, a family emergency, jury duty, or military service. We handle longer leaves case by case and in line with the law.

- **Family and medical leave.** Where federal or state law (such as the FMLA, to the extent it applies to an employer our size) entitles you to job-protected leave, we honor it. Even where formal entitlements don't apply, we try to work with people facing real medical or family situations.
- **Parental leave.** Talk to Greta or your manager as early as you reasonably can if you're expecting to add to your family, so we can plan coverage and your leave together.
- **Bereavement.** If you lose someone close to you, take the time you need to grieve and handle arrangements. Tell your manager, and we'll work it out compassionately.
- **Jury duty and civic obligations.** We support you serving on a jury or meeting legal obligations. Bring your manager the notice when you receive it.
- **Military leave.** We comply with USERRA and provide leave and reinstatement rights to employees who serve.

For any leave longer than a few days, loop in your manager and Nadia Foster early. The sooner we know, the better we can support you and keep the shop covered while you're out.

## 9. Code of Conduct

Our code of conduct is short because it comes down to a few things: be honest, be respectful, and protect the trust customers place in us. Here's what that looks like day to day.

### Treat customers honestly

- Give accurate, useful advice, even when it costs us a sale. Steering someone to the right, cheaper, or repairable option is the job.
- Quote prices, policies, and timelines accurately. If you don't know, say you'll find out — don't make something up.
- Honor our return and rental policies as written. Don't promise exceptions you're not authorized to make.

### Treat coworkers with respect

- Communicate directly and kindly. Disagree about ideas, not people.
- Don't gossip, undermine, or take credit for someone else's work.
- Pull your weight, and help when a teammate is buried.

### Protect company property and trust

- Handle inventory, cash, and the register with care and integrity. Cash-handling procedures exist for everyone's protection — follow them exactly.
- Use your employee discount honestly and only for yourself and as the policy allows (see Dress Code & Staff Gear). Never buy on your discount for someone else to resell.
- Don't take gear, supplies, or "samples" without paying or proper authorization. Theft, of any size, is grounds for immediate termination.

### Conflicts of interest

If you have an outside interest that could conflict with Trailhead — for instance, working for a competing shop, or steering customers to a business you're connected to — disclose it to your manager or Greta. Most conflicts are manageable once they're out in the open; hidden ones are the problem.

A few specifics that come up in a gear shop:

- **Vendor gifts.** Reps sometimes offer samples, swag, or perks. Small, normal items (a hat, a sticker, a demo product to actually test for the shop) are fine. Anything that could look like it's buying influence over what we stock should be declined or run past Sam Whitfield or Greta first.
- **Side work.** Guiding, instructing, repair work on the side, or selling gear privately can be fine — but if it overlaps with what Trailhead does or uses anything you learned or have access to here, talk to your manager so we keep it clean.
- **Customer relationships.** Be friendly, be helpful, and keep it professional. Don't use your position or customer information for personal gain.

### Confidentiality

In the course of your work you'll learn things that aren't public — what we pay for gear, our margins, vendor terms, sales numbers, customer lists, and the plans we're kicking around. Keep that information inside the shop. Don't share it with competitors, post it online, or take it with you if you leave. Customer information in particular is private and protected (see Technology & Acceptable Use). This obligation continues after you stop working here.

### Substances

Reporting to work, or working, under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs is prohibited. It's a safety issue around ladders, heavy gear, and sharp tools, and it's a service issue with customers. If you're struggling with substance use, we'd rather you come to us for help than hide it.

### Discipline

When something goes wrong, we generally aim to coach and correct before we escalate — a conversation, then a documented warning, then more serious steps if needed. But serious misconduct (theft, violence, harassment, working impaired, falsifying time records) can lead to immediate termination. Discipline is handled fairly and consistently, and nothing here changes the at-will nature of employment.

### How we treat customers

Customer experience isn't a separate department here; it's everyone's job, and Rosa Iglesias sets the bar for it. A few standards we hold ourselves to on the floor, on the phone, and in our email replies:

- **Greet people.** Acknowledge customers within a reasonable moment of them coming in, even if it's just a "hey, I'll be right with you." Nobody should feel ignored or hovered over.
- **Ask before you advise.** Find out what they're actually trying to do — the trip, the conditions, their experience — before recommending anything. The right gear depends on the person.
- **Be honest about fit and need.** If something isn't right for them, say so. If they don't need the expensive version, tell them. We'd rather lose a sale than a customer's trust.
- **Default to repair.** When someone brings in worn or broken gear, our first instinct is whether Lewis can fix it. "Repair, don't replace" is a customer promise, not just an internal one.
- **Own the problem.** If a customer has an issue, help them even if it isn't "your" area. Get the right person if you need to, but don't pass people around like a hot potato.
- **Handle returns gracefully.** Follow our return and rental policies as written, and bring a manager in for anything outside them. A calm, fair return turns a frustrated person into a repeat customer.

When you're unsure how to handle a tricky customer situation, get Rosa or a manager. Stepping back to bring in help is always the right call — it's never a failure.

## 10. Office Etiquette

We share a break room, a back office, and a handful of desks and work surfaces, and twelve people can make a small space messy in a hurry. Keeping our shared spaces clean is a basic courtesy that makes the workday better for everyone. The rule is simple: **leave shared spaces at least as clean as you found them.**

### The break room and kitchen

- **Clean up after yourself.** Wash your dishes — or load and run the dishwasher — don't leave them in the sink "to deal with later." Later never comes for the next person.
- **Label your food.** If you store something in the shared fridge, label it with your name and the date. Unlabeled items are fair game to be tossed on cleaning day.
- **Clear out the fridge.** We clean out the fridge every Friday afternoon. Take home what's yours before then, because anything unlabeled or clearly old gets thrown out.
- **Wipe the counters and table** after you eat. Crumbs and spills attract pests, and Pinedale has plenty of critters that would love to move in.
- **Refill and reset.** Empty the coffee pot if it's the dregs, replace the roll if you finish the paper towels, and take the trash out when it's full instead of stacking on top.

### Shared desks and the back office

- **Don't camp on a shared desk.** If a desk or workstation is shared, clear your stuff and reset it when you're done so the next person can sit down and work.
- **Keep common surfaces tidy.** The back office counter is not a permanent home for your coffee mug, your jacket, or a pile of paperwork. Put things where they belong.
- **Return what you borrow.** Tools, tape, scissors, the label printer — put them back where you found them. A shared tool that's never where it should be is a tool nobody can rely on.
- **Mind the noise.** The back office is where people take customer calls and concentrate on orders and the books. Keep it reasonably quiet and take loud conversations elsewhere.

### Restrooms and the floor

- Keep the restroom tidy and tell your manager if it needs restocking or attention.
- On the sales floor, straighten as you go — refold, restack, and reshelve. A clean floor is part of the customer experience, and it's everyone's job, not just whoever's closing.

None of this is complicated. It's just the difference between a shop that feels cared for and one that feels neglected. We're a "mind the details" kind of place, and the break room counts.

## 11. Pet Policy

We're an outdoor shop, and a lot of us share our lives — and our trails — with animals. We've made a little room for that at work, within sensible limits.

Animals are welcome in the office on Fridays, provided they are leashed and other team members consent.

That's the policy, and here's how it works in practice:

- **Fridays only.** Bringing an animal in is a Friday thing, not an every-day thing. The rest of the week, please leave pets at home.
- **Leashed and under control.** Animals stay leashed and with their owner. Your animal is your responsibility the entire time it's in the building.
- **With everyone's consent.** "Other team members consent" means exactly that — if a coworker on the floor or in the office isn't comfortable, for any reason, the animal can't be in the shared space with them. Allergies, nervousness, and personal preference all count, and nobody has to explain themselves. Check before you bring an animal around the team.
- **Behaved and clean.** Animals that come in should be calm, housetrained, and good around people. Clean up immediately after any accident. If an animal is disruptive, stressed, or making customers uneasy, it needs to go home — your manager makes that call.
- **Customer areas.** Be mindful that customers come and go, and some may be uncomfortable or allergic. Keep animals out of the way and out of food and rental-gear areas.

This is a privilege we extend because it makes Fridays a little brighter for the people it suits. If it ever stops working for the team, we'll revisit it. Use good judgment and put your coworkers' comfort first.

## 12. Dress Code & Staff Gear

We sell outdoor gear, we're on our feet, and we climb the stockroom ladder a dozen times a day. Dress for that. Our look is **casual and outdoorsy** — clean, practical, and reflective of the lifestyle we sell.

The point is to build genuine product knowledge, not to run a free rental program. Use it in that spirit.

## 15. Workplace Safety

A gear shop has real physical hazards — heavy boxes, a tall stockroom, sharp repair tools, and floors that get wet when it snows outside. Most injuries here are preventable with a little care. Your safety, your coworkers' safety, and our customers' safety come first, always.

### Lifting and carrying

- **Lift with your legs, not your back.** Bend your knees, keep the load close, and don't twist while carrying.
- **Get help with heavy or awkward loads.** Big boxes of gear, rental tents, and stacked kayaks are two-person jobs. Ask — nobody's impressed by a hurt back.
- **Use the cart or hand truck** for moving multiple boxes rather than hero-stacking them in your arms.

### Ladders and the stockroom

- **Use the right ladder or step stool** for reaching high shelves in the stockroom — never a chair, a box, or the bottom shelf.
- **Check the ladder before you climb:** feet planted, fully open and locked, on level ground.
- **Keep three points of contact** and don't overreach. Climb down and move the ladder instead of stretching sideways.
- **Don't carry heavy items up or down** the ladder with both hands full. Get a spotter or use a different method.

### Slips, trips, and floors

- **Clean up spills immediately** and put out a wet-floor sign when needed — especially near the entrance in wet or snowy weather.
- **Keep aisles and walkways clear.** Boxes, gear, and cords don't belong underfoot.
- **Wear shoes with good traction** (see Dress Code) — it matters most when the floors are wet.

### Repair bench and tools

- Use cutting tools, awls, and machines as Lewis Tran trains you, with the proper guards and protective gear.
- Keep the bench area tidy; a cluttered bench is where accidents happen.
- Report a dull, damaged, or malfunctioning tool instead of forcing it.
- Adhesives, solvents, and waterproofing treatments live at the bench for a reason — use them in a ventilated area, follow the label, and store them properly. Know where the safety data sheets are kept.

### Seasonal demands

Our busiest stretch is the warm-weather rental and visitor season, and that's exactly when safety shortcuts are most tempting. Rental gear is heavy and bulky, the stockroom turns over fast, and everyone's moving quickly. The rules above matter most precisely when it's busy. A rushed lift or an unsafe reach during a packed Saturday is how people get hurt. Slow down for the ten seconds it takes to do it right.

### Winter and the entrance

Pinedale winters bring snow, ice, and a lot of wet boots through the front door. Keep the entry mats down, mop up tracked-in slush, and salt or clear the walk outside when conditions call for it. The area right inside the door is the single most common slip spot in the shop — stay on top of it.

### Emergencies and incident reporting

- Know where the fire extinguishers, exits, and first-aid kit are. If you don't, ask your manager today.
- In a true emergency, get yourself and others to safety first, then call 911.
- **Report every injury, near-miss, or unsafe condition to your manager as soon as it happens** — even small ones, and even if no one was hurt. We document incidents so we can fix the cause and so any needed medical care and paperwork are handled properly. Reporting a safety problem will never get you in trouble; hiding one might.

## 16. Anti-Harassment & Respectful Workplace

Everyone at Trailhead has the right to work in an environment free of harassment, discrimination, and intimidation. We mean that plainly, and we enforce it.

We do not tolerate harassment based on race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, age, disability, genetic information, veteran status, or any other protected characteristic. This includes, but isn't limited to:

- Unwelcome sexual advances, comments about someone's body or appearance, or requests for sexual favors.
- Displaying or sharing offensive images or messages.
- Bullying, threats, or intimidation, whether in person, in writing, or online.
- Retaliation against someone for reporting a concern or taking part in an investigation.

### No retaliation

We will not retaliate against anyone who reports a concern in good faith or who participates in an investigation, and we won't allow anyone else to. ## 17. Technology & Acceptable Use

We run on a handful of shared systems — the point-of-sale (POS), our e-commerce and inventory tools, email, and the computers in the back office. Use them responsibly. They hold customer information and keep the shop running.

### Company devices and the POS

- **Company devices and accounts are for work.** A little incidental personal use is fine, but the POS, the office computers, and company logins are work tools first.
- **Keep your logins yours.** Don't share passwords, and don't let someone ring up sales or place orders under your account. The register and POS are tied to you for a reason.
- **Lock it when you step away.** Don't leave a logged-in POS or office computer open and unattended where a customer could reach it.
- **Handle the register by the book.** Cash-handling and refund procedures protect you and the shop — follow them exactly, every time.

### Wifi

We provide **"Trailhead-Guest"** as the wifi network for customers in the shop. Feel free to point customers to it by name when they ask. For your own work, use the systems and network your manager sets you up with during onboarding — don't reconfigure shop networking or connect unapproved devices to internal systems without IT or manager approval.

### Data care

- **Protect customer information.** Names, contact details, and payment information are private. Don't share, export, screenshot, or take home customer data, and never discuss specific customers' information outside of doing your job.
- **Think before you click.** Don't open suspicious email attachments or links, and report anything phishy to your manager. A single bad click can put customer data at risk.
- **Don't install random software** on company computers without approval, and don't plug in unknown USB drives.
- **Personal social media is your own** — but don't post customer information, internal numbers, or confidential shop business, and don't speak for the company unless that's part of your role (that's Hana Kim's lane). Be decent online; you represent Trailhead even off the clock when people know where you work.

If something breaks or seems compromised — a system acting strange, a possible data exposure, a lost device — tell your manager right away. Fast reporting limits the damage.

## 18. Leaving Trailhead / Returning Equipment

People move on, and that's okay. Whether you're leaving for a new chapter or it's the end of a season, we want your last days here to be as smooth and respectful as your first.

### Giving notice

If you're resigning, we appreciate **at least two weeks' written notice** to your manager. It lets us plan coverage and wrap things up well, and it helps us part on good terms — Pinedale is a small town, and references matter.

### Your final paycheck

You'll receive your final pay, including any wages owed and any accrued, unused PTO that's payable under our policy and Wyoming law, in accordance with applicable law. Nadia Foster handles your final pay; if you have questions about it, she's your contact. Make sure your address and direct-deposit details are current so nothing gets lost.

### Returning company property

On or before your last day, return everything that belongs to Trailhead. That typically includes:

- **Keys and access** — store keys, alarm codes (which we'll deactivate), and any access fobs.
- **Devices and equipment** — any company laptop, tablet, phone, or tools issued to you.
- **Uniform and branded apparel** that was provided rather than purchased, where applicable, along with name tags.
- **Documents and data** — return or properly hand off any work materials, and delete any company data from personal devices.

We'll disable your logins to the POS, e-commerce, email, and other systems as part of your offboarding. Don't try to log in after you've left.

Any outstanding company property not returned may, where the law allows, be deducted from your final pay or otherwise recovered. We'd much rather just get the keys back, though.

### Leaving well

Tell us how we did. We genuinely want to hear what worked and what didn't, so a brief exit conversation with your manager or Greta is always welcome. And the door isn't closed behind you — plenty of good people leave and come back, and former teammates are always welcome to stop in, say hi, and shop the floor.

Thanks for being part of Trailhead. However long you were here, you helped people get outside, and that's work worth being proud of.
